The idea

What is a mind map?

Instead of writing notes in one long list, a mind map spreads your ideas out like branches of a tree. Related ideas group together, and your brain can actually see how everything connects.

🎨
Colors
Use a different color for each branch. It helps your brain group and separate ideas at a glance.
✏️
Keywords
Write short keywords only. No full sentences. Your brain fills in the rest when you review.
🖼️
Pictures
Add a small image to each branch. Visuals make information stick way longer than words alone.

Why does this work? Your brain remembers pictures and patterns much better than lists of words. Mind maps use both — so you remember more with less effort.

Build one

How to build a mind map

Every mind map follows the same 4-step structure. Get these right and yours will look professional every time.

1
Start with your main idea in the center
Write your whole topic in the middle of the page inside a circle or bubble. This is your anchor — everything else connects back to it.
For your assignment: write Computer Parts in the center.
2
Branch out your main topics
Draw 4 lines out from the center — one for each main category. Each branch gets its own color. Write one keyword on each line.
Think: what are the 4 biggest categories from your outline notes?
3
Add facts under each topic
From each branch, draw 2 to 3 smaller lines. On each one, write a short fact — just 2 to 5 words. No full sentences. Keywords only.
If you need 10 words to say it, cut it to 4. Your brain will fill in the rest.
4
Add a picture to each branch
Draw or paste a small image near each main branch. It does not have to be perfect — even a stick figure helps your brain remember the information.
For the assignment: use clip art in Canva. One image per branch.
Good vs bad

Strong vs Weak mind maps

Two students mapped the same notes about the four seasons. One is useful. One is pointless. Can you spot the difference?

Strong — keeps the details

Each branch has 2 short facts. If you lost the original notes, this map still teaches you something.

Weak — just single words

Only branch labels, no details. A week later you will have no idea what you were supposed to learn. This is just a list with extra steps.

The rule: If you lost your original notes and only had the mind map, could you still understand the topic? If yes — it is strong. If no — add more facts.

Now do it in Canva

Take your Computer Parts outline notes and turn them into a mind map. Here is exactly what to do:

1
Open Canva and start a Landscape Flyer template.
2
Put Computer Parts in a bubble in the center of the page.
3
Branch out your 4 main topics from your outline notes — one per branch, each a different color.
4
Add 2 to 3 key facts (short keywords, not sentences) under each branch.
5
Add one clip-art image near each branch. Black and white only — no photos, no color images.

Before you submit — ask yourself:

  • If I lost my outline notes, could I still understand Computer Parts from this map alone?
  • Did I use keywords only — no full sentences anywhere?
  • Does each branch have a different color?
  • Is there one clip-art image per branch?
